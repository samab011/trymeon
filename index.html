[ 02 ] How it goes

Four weeks from brief to live.

  1. W1

    Map

    Workshop, audit, and a written plan: what the site must do, page by page, and how we'll know it worked.

    Deliverable Build spec + sitemap
  2. W2

    Design

    Art direction, type system, and the key pages designed to the real content — not lorem ipsum.

    Deliverable Design system + screens
  3. W3

    Motion & build

    Front-end build with the motion cut in from the start, so animation is part of the page, not bolted on.

    Deliverable Staging site + assets
  4. W4

    Launch & support

    Ship it, wire the AI support layer to your real docs, and hand over the keys. Then we stay on call.

    Deliverable Live site + support desk

[ 05 ] Questions

The useful answers.

Do you replace our developers?

No. We plan, design and produce — and we build front-end when you want us to. If you have an engineering team, we hand over a spec and a component library they'll actually enjoy using.

What does "AI support" actually mean here?

Two things. First, an assistant on your site and inbox trained on your real documentation, with strict rules about what it may answer and when it must escalate. Second, us — a human team on call behind it, reviewing what the assistant couldn't handle.

How heavy is the motion?

Budgeted per page before we shoot a frame. Loops are compressed, lazy-loaded and paused off-screen, and every sequence has a reduced-motion fallback. Typical page stays under 400kb of media on mobile.

Can we start small?

Yes — Sprint exists for that. Most clients start with a plan or a motion pack and stack add-ons once they've seen the work.

Who owns the output?

You do. Files, source, tokens and prompts are handed over at the end of every engagement, no exit fee.
